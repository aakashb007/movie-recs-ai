# Five-person usability test

Goal: can someone who has never seen the app find something they'd actually watch tonight?

**Who:** 5 people who watch at least one movie or series a week. Mix of ages; at least one
uses TalkBack or large text, and at least one mostly watches series.

**Setup:** fresh install with a deployed backend on their own phone if possible. Screen + voice
recording with consent. You observe; you don't help.

## Tasks (say them, don't show them)
1. "Set the app up so it knows what you like." *(onboarding)*
2. "Find something to watch tonight that you haven't seen." *(home feed)*
3. "Tell the app about one suggestion you'd never watch." *(long-press → Not for me)*
4. "You want something like a show you love, but lighter. Ask for it." *(Ask)*
5. "Find out where you could watch one of these." *(details → where to watch)*
6. "Save something for the weekend." *(watchlist)*

## Record for each person
| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Time from first launch to naming a title they'd genuinely watch | < 2 min |
| Tasks completed without help | 6 / 6 |
| Picks they've already seen in the first 10 | ≤ 3 |
| "Why is this here?" — can they say why a pick was suggested (from the reason line) | 5 / 5 people |
| One-word reaction to the For you page | — |

## Afterwards
- Ask: "Was any recommendation embarrassing or obviously wrong? Which?"
- Ask: "When you typed your request, did the app understand you?" (Check the chips it showed.)
- Note every hesitation longer than ~5 s and where it happened.

Fix anything that 2 or more people hit before shipping.
